Why Is Everyone Talking About a Dyson Toothbrush with a Camera?

Dyson announced CameraJet on September 1, 2026, introducing a toothbrush built around what it calls Gap Optical Targeting.

A small 100,000-pixel macro camera analyzes the mouth in real time while software identifies interdental gaps. When the system detects a gap, it can trigger a broad, cone-shaped jet of mouthrinse or water toward that space. The MyDyson app can also provide live viewing, guided cleaning, coverage mapping, and session feedback.

That combination is what makes the product unusual. A normal electric toothbrush focuses mainly on brushing. A separate water flosser focuses on spaces between teeth. CameraJet tries to bring both jobs into one routine.

The Behavior Problem It Is Trying to Solve

Many people struggle to make interdental cleaning a consistent daily habit. Some dislike string floss. Others find it awkward, forget to do it, or simply stop once the routine becomes inconvenient.

CameraJet's most interesting idea may therefore be less about “AI dentistry” and more about habit design: can combining brushing and interdental cleaning make people more consistent?

What About Privacy?

A camera inside a toothbrush naturally raises another question: where do those images go? Dyson says images processed by the device or MyDyson app are not stored or shared, and that Dyson or third parties cannot access them.

How Does the Dyson CameraJet Work?

The easiest way to understand CameraJet is to think of it as three familiar tools combined into one handle.

CameraJet component What it is designed to do What it does not mean
Electric brush head Cleans exposed tooth surfaces and along the gumline. It cannot remove hardened tartar after it has formed.
100,000-pixel camera Helps identify interdental gaps and provides live viewing. It is not a dental X-ray or professional diagnosis.
Targeted liquid jet Sends mouthrinse or water toward spaces between teeth. It does not make one interdental-cleaning method right for everyone.
MyDyson app Provides guided cleaning, coverage mapping, and session feedback. App feedback cannot replace a clinical examination.

Dyson also says its conical jet is designed differently from the narrow “needle-type” stream used by many traditional water flossers. According to Dyson, the broader spray was engineered to sweep a wider area while using lower pressure.

Those are manufacturer claims, so they are best understood as part of Dyson's product testing—not as a reason to assume every patient should change their current routine.

Can Dyson CameraJet Replace Flossing?

This is the main question behind the viral interest.

Dyson uses the term “precision-flossing” for its targeted jet system. The company also cites clinical and laboratory testing for plaque-removal and gum-health claims.

That is stronger evidence than a simple marketing slogan. But it still does not mean CameraJet should automatically replace every person's existing interdental-cleaning method.

The American Dental Association recommends cleaning between teeth every day and recognizes several ways to do it, including traditional dental floss, interdental brushes, special picks, powered interdental cleaners, and water flossers.

The better question“What is the easiest method I will use consistently, and does it clean the spaces in my mouth effectively?”

Someone who already flosses comfortably every day may see CameraJet mainly as a convenience upgrade. Someone who repeatedly skips interdental cleaning because they dislike string floss may find an integrated liquid-jet system easier to maintain.

Can the Camera Spot Cavities or Gum Disease?

No—not in the way a dental examination can.

CameraJet's camera is intended for gap detection, live viewing, and cleaning guidance. It does not turn the toothbrush into a diagnostic device.

A dental diagnosis may involve a visual and tactile examination, dental X-rays when appropriate, periodontal measurements, symptom history, bite testing, and other clinical information depending on the concern.

That difference matters because seeing something clearly is not the same as understanding what it is. A dark groove might be staining, decay, or simply normal anatomy. Red gums might be irritated for several different reasons. A camera feed cannot make that distinction for you.

If you have pain, swelling, persistent bleeding, sensitivity, a loose restoration, or a spot that concerns you, it is better to have the area evaluated rather than relying on a home camera. Explore common dental conditions treated at DDD NYC.

Before You Buy: Three Practical Things Worth Knowing

Water or mouthrinse?

Dyson says the tank can use mouthrinse or water. For best camera performance, it recommends low-foaming/SLS-free products because excessive foam can interfere with visibility.

Brush-head replacement?

Dyson says each brush head is engineered for about three months of use, with RFID monitoring to alert the user when replacement is due.

Tank cleaning?

Dyson instructs users to rinse the brush head and tank after use, clear remaining liquid from the nozzle, rinse the device, and allow the parts to air-dry.

What Should Patients Take Away from the CameraJet Launch?

The most useful part of this launch may not be the word AI. It is the attention being placed on a very ordinary but important part of oral health: cleaning between your teeth consistently.

Smart home-care technology can be helpful when it makes a healthy habit easier to maintain. The problem starts when technology creates false confidence that a device has “checked” your mouth or replaced professional evaluation.

  • Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between your teeth every day using a method suited to your mouth.
  • Do not expect a home toothbrush to remove hardened tartar.
  • Use apps and cameras as feedback—not as diagnosis.
  • See your dentist at the interval recommended for your individual oral-health needs.

Dyson CameraJet: Common Questions

Is the Dyson CameraJet toothbrush real?

Yes. Dyson officially announced CameraJet on September 1, 2026. It combines an electric toothbrush, a macro camera, software-based gap detection, MyDyson app connectivity, and a targeted liquid jet.

How much does the Dyson CameraJet cost?

Can CameraJet replace traditional floss?

It is designed to clean between teeth with a targeted liquid jet, but there is no single interdental-cleaning method that is best for every patient. The ADA recommends daily interdental cleaning and recognizes several tools, including floss, interdental brushes, and water flossers.

Can the CameraJet camera detect cavities?

No. The camera is intended for live viewing, gap detection, and cleaning guidance. A cavity or gum problem may require a clinical examination, X-rays when appropriate, and other professional assessment.

Dyson says images processed by the device or MyDyson app are not stored or shared.
